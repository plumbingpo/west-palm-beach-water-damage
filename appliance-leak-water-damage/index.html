Appliance Leak Water Damage in West Palm Beach: What to Know

Most of the appliance leaks we see trace back to three machines. Washer supply hoses can burst and keep spraying at full pressure until the valve is closed. A dishwasher tends to leak slowly from a door seal or drain hose, out of sight under the cabinets. The ice maker line on a refrigerator is a thin tube, and it can drip for months behind the unit. Each one needs a different kind of cleanup.

On every West Palm Beach job, the first step is moving the appliance and tracing the path the water took. Kitchen leaks tend to end up beneath the cabinets and underneath the floor. In laundry rooms it means the wall behind the machine and the rooms next door, or the ceiling below if the laundry is upstairs. Standing water is extracted, absorbed moisture is measured, and drying equipment is sized to fit the wet area.

Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Shut the Machine DownCancel the cycle, and pull the plug only when the floor and the outlet near it are dry. If not, switch it off at the breaker instead.
  2. Close the Supply ValveWashers have hot and cold valves on the wall behind them. Dishwashers shut off under the sink. Refrigerator lines have a small valve behind or below.
  3. Soak Up Standing WaterGetting reachable water up with a mop or towels stops it from moving under the cabinets and the floor. Vacuums are safe for this only when built for wet and dry pickup.
  4. Keep the Door ClosedDo not open a washer or dishwasher that is still full. When the door opens mid-cycle, the full load of water can pour out on the floor.
  5. Document What FailedTake pictures of the hose, valve or fitting that failed and keep the part. Both the appliance maker and your insurer may want to look at it.

Will Insurance Pay?

What About Insurance?

When a machine overflows unexpectedly or a hose bursts, that is typically treated as sudden and accidental, which many homeowners policies cover. Payment typically goes toward the damage the water did, and the appliance or the part that failed is not included. A claim is harder when an ice maker line or dishwasher seeped slowly, since coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks. Some insurers also ask about the age and upkeep of the hose. Hold on to the part that failed, photograph everything ahead of cleanup, and review what your policy says.

The Usual Culprits

Why This Happens

  • A dishwasher with a failed pump seal, a cracked drain hose or a worn door gasket.
  • Installation mistakes like a loose supply line connection or a dishwasher that does not sit level.
  • A kinked or cracked ice maker line, or one whose fitting has worked loose.
  • Standpipes that clog, or the drain hose from a washer coming loose from one.
  • Rubber washing machine supply hoses that weaken with age and split under pressure.

What the Water Damages

Flooring and Subfloor

Hardwood, vinyl plank and laminate all let water slip underneath. Cupping and swelling show up in the boards, and the subfloor can stay damp long after the surface appears dry.

Cabinet Damage

Dishwasher leaks soak cabinet sides and toe kicks from underneath. Swelling and separation follow, and for weeks the soaked cabinet boxes may trap moisture against the wall.

Baseboards and Wall Bottoms

Water standing on a laundry or kitchen floor wicks up into baseboards and the bottom of the drywall. Often the worst of it is on the wall in back of the appliance, where it went unseen.

Rooms Next Door

For hours, a failed washer hose can keep releasing water. That water moves beneath walls into bedrooms, closets and hallways, soaking pad and carpet far from the laundry room.
