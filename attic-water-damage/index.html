Attic Water Damage in West Palm Beach

Attic water damage comes from two directions. Worn out shingles, flashing that has failed, and the seals around chimneys and vents all let rain in from overhead. The house itself sends moisture upward too, and in a poorly ventilated attic it condenses on cold sheathing. In both cases, almost nobody climbs up to check. Our team in West Palm Beach inspects the attic, finds the source, and dries it out.

Most attic jobs center on wet insulation. Fiberglass and cellulose both lose R-value when they get wet, so the home costs more to heat and cool. The bigger problem is that for weeks, saturated insulation holds moisture on the drywall of the ceiling and on the framing. Saturated material comes out, air movers and dehumidifiers dry the wood, and any mold on the sheathing or rafters gets treated.

Warning Signs

  • Light from outside visible along a roof valley or around a chimney or vent pipe
  • An upstairs ceiling where the paint has blistered or started to peel
  • Fuzzy white or black growth on the sheathing, usually thickest by the eaves
  • Stains or drips where a light fixture or exhaust fan meets the ceiling
  • Dripping, rusted or water marked ducts and metal hardware up in the attic
  • Rafters and roof decking marked with dark streaks or stains
Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Inspect and Trace

Our inspection covers the flashing points, roof deck, exhaust ducts and vents, and tells us if the water is condensation, a leak, or both. How far it spread is shown by moisture meters and thermal imaging.

2

Remove Wet Insulation

Insulation that is soaked or has mold gets bagged and carried out, giving the wood and the drywall below a chance to dry. The access path is kept contained and the living area underneath is protected.

3

Dry the Wood

Rafters, sheathing and joists are dried by air movers and dehumidifiers until their moisture levels are normal. An antimicrobial goes on after any visible mold has been cleaned from the wood.

4

Fix the Cause

You are shown the ventilation or roof problem to be corrected, and our findings can go to your roofer. Once the attic stays dry, new insulation goes in to restore the R-value.

If Nothing Is Done

The Damage Timeline

First storm

Insulation Soaks It Up

Rain that makes it through the roof falls into the insulation and is absorbed. Because the rooms below show nothing, the leak is missed and no roof repair happens.

24 to 48 hours

Mold May Begin

In a still, warm attic, paper facing and wood that stay damp give mold ideal conditions. It can start on the back side of the ceiling drywall and on the sheathing.

A few weeks on

Stains Come Through

Once storm after storm has saturated the insulation, the ceiling starts getting wet. In the room underneath, brown rings show up and the drywall may begin sagging.

After months

Rot Sets In

Roofing nails loosen as the sheathing delaminates. Rafters stay damp enough to decay, and mold covers large areas of the roof deck.

Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Mold on Sheathing

The underside of the roof deck is a common place for mold when ventilation is poor. A wide area may be covered by the time somebody climbs up to check.

Insulation That Stops Working

Compressed, damp insulation does very little insulating, and it may not recover after it dries. Energy bills climb, and the rooms below the wet area are hotter or colder than the rest.

Bath Fans Venting Inside

Some bathroom fans exhaust into the attic and never reach the outdoors, adding warm, damp air daily. The condensation it leaves on the sheathing can pass for a roof leak.

A Falling Ceiling

Saturated insulation is heavy. Drywall that has been wet from above weakens at the fasteners and can drop suddenly, bringing wet insulation down with it.
