Hardwood Floor Water Damage Help in West Palm Beach

Hardwood responds fast when it gets wet. Boards take on moisture from below and from the edges, swell against each other and start to cup, with the edges rising higher than the center. That does not mean the floor is lost. When drying starts within the first few days, solid hardwood frequently lies flat again. What matters is removing moisture from the wood and from its subfloor steadily and under control.

Drying mats are how our team handles wet wood floors. Each mat is sealed against the floor and hooked to a vacuum unit, which pulls moisture upward through seams and boards as dehumidifiers dry the air in the room. This takes longer than drying carpet or drywall, often a week or more. No sanding should happen before the wood has dried completely. Sand a cupped floor too soon and it will crown later.

Warning Signs

  • A hazy, whitened or flaking finish where the water sat
  • Gaps opening between boards after a floor has dried too fast
  • Creaking and popping noises from boards that used to be quiet
  • Boards that feel soft or spongy underfoot or flex more than before
  • Nail heads and seams ringed by dark staining
  • A musty smell rising from the floor with nothing visible on top

Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Drying Too Fast

High heat or too much airflow pulls moisture from the surface faster than the core can follow. Boards may then split, crack or shrink apart for good.

Rusting Fasteners

Lingering moisture rusts the fasteners, which then hold the boards less firmly. It can leave black staining around each fastener that goes deeper than a sanding reaches.

Mold Under Boards

The space between hardwood and subfloor is dark and has no airflow. Growth there is invisible from above and shows up first as a musty smell.

Damage Beyond the Room

Moisture beneath flooring does not stay put. It can reach under cabinets, wall plates and into neighboring rooms, or drip through to the ceiling of the level beneath.

The Clock

The Damage Timeline

The first few hours

Water Goes Under

Water slips through the seams and spreads between the boards and subfloor. You can towel off the top and still have a saturated underside.

24 to 48 hours

Edges Begin to Rise

Swelling sets in and board edges lift. A cloudy look can appear in the finish. The bare underside of the boards and the subfloor are where mold can start.

Days 3 through 7

Pressure Builds Up

Swelling builds pressure across the floor. With fasteners losing their hold and boards compressing at the edges, some areas can lift away from the subfloor.

After a week

Permanent Damage

Black stains, buckled boards and mold beneath the flooring are now probable. By now replacement, not drying, is the answer for most of the affected section.

What We Do

What Happens After You Call

1

Measure the Moisture

Our team meters the wet boards, the subfloor under them and a dry area of floor to compare against. That tells us how deep the water went and whether saving the floor is realistic.

2

Remove Surface Water

Rugs and furnishings come off the affected boards while pooled water is vacuumed up at once. Cutting off the water entering the joints early keeps swelling down.

3

Install the Mat System

A vacuum system hooked to mats sealed on the surface lifts moisture out of the boards from above. Dehumidifiers control the room so the wood dries evenly without cracking.

4

Track Progress and Report

We track readings every day or two until boards and subfloor match dry reference areas. At that point you hear from us if it needs nothing, some replacement boards, or a refinish.
