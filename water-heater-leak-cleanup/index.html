Water Heater Leak Cleanup in West Palm Beach: What to Know

Even though the heater itself contains 40 to 80 gallons, the leak is not capped at that amount. Because the supply line feeds the tank, a rusted out bottom drains and refills at the same pace. It will run until the cold water valve gets closed. The location of the heater sets the damage. It may be a hallway closet, a garage floor or, the worst case, an attic over finished rooms.

In West Palm Beach, we start with water extraction and then inspect everything around the heater. Closet walls and the platform under the tank are usually soaked at the bottom. In a garage, water reaches the wall shared with the house and anything stored on the floor. From an attic it comes down through insulation and ceilings. Each of those areas is metered, then dried using dehumidifiers and air movers.

Right Now

What to Do Right Now

  1. Turn Off the Heat SourceElectric units get shut down at the breaker. With a gas heater, the knob on the control goes to the off position. A burner or heating element must not stay on once the tank is empty.
  2. Shut the Cold Water ValveTurn the valve on the cold pipe above the heater clockwise. A stuck or leaking valve means going to the house main and shutting the water off there.
  3. Watch for Hot WaterTank water is hot enough to scald. Let pooled water cool off before anyone steps in it, and keep kids and pets out of the area.
  4. Empty the Tank OutsideWhen the drain valve is within reach, connect a garden hose and lead it to a floor drain or outdoors, which keeps the rest of the water off the floor.
  5. Move Stored ItemsGet rugs, boxes and other absorbent things up off the wet floor. Because cardboard and fabric take in water quickly, they hold dampness against the walls and the slab.
Will Insurance Pay?

What About Insurance?

Water damage to belongings, walls and floors from a tank that fails suddenly is often covered as a sudden and accidental loss. The water heater itself usually is not, since wearing out with age is considered maintenance. Rust trails, old staining or other evidence that the leak was long-term can lead an adjuster to reduce or deny the claim, since gradual leaks usually fall outside coverage. Check your policy, photograph the heater and the damage, and keep the old unit until the claim is settled.

Why It Happens

What Causes It

  • A drain valve that loosens, cracks or no longer closes all the way.
  • A blocked or rusted drain pan under an attic or closet heater that overflows instead of draining.
  • An aging steel tank whose bottom is eaten through by corrosion and sediment.
  • Corroded inlet and outlet fittings or flex connectors on top of the heater.
  • A temperature and pressure relief valve that opens or fails and discharges water.
What Is at Risk

What Happens if You Wait

Adjacent Room Flooring

Water leaving a heater closet runs under the door and into carpet, laminate or hardwood in the hall. Hot water speeds up swelling in wood flooring and loosens carpet backing.

Ceilings Under an Attic Heater

Water from an attic unit runs down through insulation to the ceiling beneath it. Insulation that is wet no longer insulates, and the added weight can pull the ceiling drywall down.

Closet Walls

Water wicks up the drywall around an interior heater closet and into the rooms on the other side. Baseboards swell and the lower wall stays wet inside.

Platform and Subfloor

The wooden stand or subfloor under the tank takes the most water. It can rot and weaken, which matters when a new full tank is set on it.

What Happens After You Call

We remove standing water from the floor, closet or garage and, for attic units, from the overflow pan and ceiling cavity, so the surrounding materials stop absorbing it.

Damaged baseboards, drywall that has started to crumble and saturated insulation all get removed. Attic jobs include removing wet insulation to expose the joists and the ceiling drywall for drying.

We keep the drying equipment going for the usual 3 to 5 days needed to dry the space. Our crew logs the readings and lets you know as soon as the area is ready for a replacement heater.
