Wet Carpet Drying in West Palm Beach

A wet carpet is hiding a lot more water than you can see. Up top the fibers may seem barely damp, yet the pad has filled up the way a sponge does and the subfloor under it has gotten wet as well. A shop vacuum and a stack of towels only deal with the surface. In West Palm Beach, we pull the water out, take moisture meter readings on each layer, and place drying equipment that works down to the subfloor.

Two things decide if a carpet is worth saving: the cleanliness of the water and the time it has sat wet. Carpet soaked by a clean supply line can usually be dried in place if we get to it quickly. With an appliance leak, which is gray water, we often discard the pad, then clean and treat the carpet. Sewage or outside flood water means carpet and pad both come out.

What It Looks Like

  • A sour, musty smell that gets stronger when the room is closed up
  • Dark, rusty lines along the wall where the tack strip has gotten wet
  • Swelling in the baseboards, or bubbled paint just above them
  • Loose, wavy sections where the tack strip no longer holds the carpet
  • Yellow or brown discoloration rising to the fiber tips while the carpet dries
  • Lift a corner and the backing peels away from the carpet
Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

First hours

Water Moves Outward

Under the surface, the pad pulls water outward, well beyond what looks wet. It slips beneath walls into neighboring rooms while the tack strip begins to absorb it.

Hours 24 to 48

Odor and Mold Begin

That sour wet carpet odor shows up as bacteria multiply in the damp pad. Mold can begin growing on the backing and on dust trapped deep in the fibers.

Days 3 through 7

Carpet Starts Failing

Ripples appear and the carpet delaminates because the latex in its backing is breaking down. Rust forms on the tack strip nails while the subfloor underneath swells.

Past one week

Too Late to Save

At this stage, removing both carpet and pad is usually the only option. Often mold has spread to the subfloor and the base of the drywall, which adds to the job.

What We Do

How We Handle Wet Carpet Drying

1

Map the Wet Area

Thermal imaging and meter readings show us where the water went, closets and the space under walls included. Then we determine which category of water it is, as that tells us what is safe to keep.

2

Extract the Water

With weighted extractors, carpet and the pad beneath it are pressed and emptied in one pass. A wand by itself leaves much more behind, so this step cuts the drying time afterward.

3

Dry Every Layer

Depending on the job, we float the carpet on moving air or lift it and remove the pad. Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on until the subfloor and the carpet each test dry.

4

Clean and Reset

Where needed, an antimicrobial goes on the subfloor and carpet, then new pad is laid if the old was removed, and the carpet is stretched back on its tack strip and cleaned.

Hidden Risks

What Hides Behind It

Mold Under the Carpet

Mold tends to start underneath, on the carpet back, because that side stays damp and dark. The odor arrives a long time ahead of any visible sign on top.

Water Quality Drops

Even clean water goes bad the longer it sits. It collects bacteria and dirt from the pad and fibers, which can turn a simple drying job into a removal.

Damp Base of the Walls

The base of each wall is where water moving beneath the carpet ends up. Drywall and baseboards wick it upward, and nobody notices because the carpet covers the edge.

Furniture Marks

When carpet is wet, the finish on wooden legs and the rust from metal glides transfer into it. Such spots are frequently there for good, while the legs soak up water and swell.
