Wall and Drywall Water Damage in West Palm Beach

A sheet of drywall behaves much as a wick does. Paper and gypsum pull water up from the floor line, so the wet zone frequently ends a foot or more past the highest point the water itself touched. Insulation and the rear of the panel can be wet through while the paint out front shows nothing unusual. For that reason our team relies on moisture meter readings rather than appearance.

Not every wet wall has to be torn out. Drywall that got wet with clean water and is still firm can often be dried in place, sometimes by removing the baseboard and moving air through small holes into the cavity. When the wall is soft, crumbling, moldy or was soaked by dirty water, we make a flood cut, a straight line above the damage, and remove everything below it.

Warning Signs

  • Drywall seams ridging up and screw pops appearing across the surface
  • Drywall that feels soft, spongy or crumbles when pressed
  • Bubbling, wrinkled or peeling paint and wallpaper low on the wall
  • Dark spots or fuzzy growth at corners and behind furniture
  • Caulk lines cracking or gaps opening where trim meets the wall
  • Baseboards that swell, warp or pull away from the wall

Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

The first few hours

Water Starts Climbing

The bottom edge of the drywall takes in water from the floor and it begins moving up. The trim and the framing plate at floor level get wet too.

Hours 24 to 48

Wall Swells

The wet line rises higher, baseboards swell and paint begins to blister. Mold can start on the paper facing inside the cavity.

Day 3 to 7

Drywall Breaks Down

Gypsum softens and the paper lets go. Insulation slumps inside the wall, odor builds and mold spreads across the hidden back side.

After a week

Framing Is Affected

By this point the studs and plates have been damp long enough for mold and early rot. A far larger section of wall needs tearing out and rebuilding.

Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Growth Behind the Paint

Paper lining, darkness and stagnant air make the hidden side of a wall a perfect place for mold. It can cover the hidden side while the painted face looks clean.

Wet Sheathing Outside

Outside walls have sheathing past the insulation that can also get soaked. That layer dries slowly and is checked before the wall is closed.

Electrical Hazards Low Down

Outlets and wiring are mounted near the floor, exactly where the water gathers. Do not use outlets on a wet wall until the circuit has been checked.

Soaked Batts in the Wall

Once wet, fiberglass insulation stays wet and keeps the studs damp. No amount of airflow on the painted side will dry a cavity packed with wet batts.

How We Fix It

Our Wall and Drywall Water Damage Process

1

Map the Moisture

Using meter readings and thermal images, our team outlines where the water went, upward and sideways, on both faces of the wall and in neighboring rooms.

2

Sort Save From Remove

Our call on each section, dry it or take it out, depends on water category, exposure time and the condition of the panel, and we explain it in plain terms.

3

Cut or Ventilate

For walls being saved, baseboards come off and air is directed into the cavity. Walls that cannot be kept get a flood cut along a level line, with the wet insulation pulled.

4

Dry and Rebuild

Air movers and dehumidifiers run until studs and remaining drywall read dry, typically 3 to 5 days. Then we insulate, hang and finish new drywall, reinstall trim and paint.
