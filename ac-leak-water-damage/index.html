Dealing With AC Leak Water Damage in West Palm Beach

Cooling a house also dries the air, which means the unit is constantly making water it must get rid of. A pan under the coil catches the drips, and from there a thin pipe empties the pan. Algae and dust can plug the line, and then the pan fills up and overflows. Attic and closet air handlers send the spill directly into the surrounding ceiling, walls or flooring.

A slow drip causes more harm than most homeowners would guess. It soaks the stand the unit sits on, climbs into the drywall and spreads beneath the flooring, and by the time you see a mark mold has frequently begun. Tracking the full extent of the moisture, drying it and making repairs is our part. Clearing the drain or repairing the unit remains a job for an HVAC technician, and our schedule can fit around theirs.

Right Now

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut the System DownNo more condensate forms once the AC is turned off at the thermostat. If water is near the air handler's wiring, turn off its breaker as well.
  2. Keep Clear of Wet ElectricalCeiling lights, outlets and the unit itself are off limits while wet, and stay that way until their circuit has been turned off.
  3. Empty the Drain PanUse a wet-vac or rags to take the standing water out of the pan and stop the spill, provided the pan is safe to reach. Skip this step if it means crossing a soaked attic.
  4. Soak Up What You CanMove clothes, boxes and furniture back from the stained area or the closet, catch ceiling drips in a bucket, wipe up hard floors and blot the carpet.
  5. Call Both TradesAn HVAC technician should clear the drain line, and our team should find out where the water went. Leave the system off until it is fixed.
Will Insurance Pay?

Does Insurance Cover It?

With AC leaks, insurers mostly look at how long the water was running. Water damage is paid for under many homeowners policies when the overflow was sudden and you responded as soon as you discovered it. Denial is possible when the adjuster concludes the leak ran for weeks or months, since that reads as a gradual or maintenance problem. Either way, the cost of fixing the AC unit is typically left out. Check your policy, and we can supply readings and photos for the claim.

Common Causes

How It Usually Starts

  • A dirty filter or low refrigerant, both of which make the coil ice up.
  • More meltwater than the pan can handle after an iced-over coil thaws.
  • Drain pans that sit unevenly or have corroded and split.
  • Sweating ducts and pipes in a humid attic wetting the drywall under them.
  • Failure of the small pump that lifts condensate to a drain.
If It Is Left

Where the Damage Spreads

Closet Walls and Platform

In a closet, the unit usually rests on a wooden stand built above the return air box. Water rots that platform and wicks up the surrounding drywall, where it stays hidden behind the unit.

Drywall Below the Air Handler

An attic air handler leaks onto the drywall beneath it. The ceiling stains first, then sags, and a section soaked for days can fall into the hallway or bedroom below.

Mold Near the Return

Within 24 to 48 hours mold can start on wet wood and drywall next to the unit, and the return may draw spores into the ducts and around the home.

Attic Insulation

Overflow in the attic sinks into the surrounding insulation, which keeps the drywall under it wet. Once saturated it stops insulating, and replacement is common.
