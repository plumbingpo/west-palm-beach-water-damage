Dealing With Roof Leak Water Damage in West Palm Beach

A roof leak rarely starts where the stain appears. Water enters at a raised shingle, a split in the flashing or a worn skylight seal, follows the rafters and decking downhill, and finally lands on the insulation. A ceiling stain tends to arrive late, well after the space overhead first got wet. Locating all of the moisture and drying it is our job.

We are a water damage crew, not a roofing company. Inside the home, our team takes out saturated insulation, dries the wood and drywall, treats any mold and repairs the ceiling. If rain is still coming in, we can tarp the roof as a temporary cover, or work alongside the roofer you choose so the leak is fixed before we close anything up.

First Five Minutes

Before We Arrive

  1. Shut Off Wet CircuitsWater coming through a ceiling fan, a light or close to an outlet means that circuit should be turned off at the panel, and the fixture should not be touched.
  2. Contain the WaterPut a pan or bucket beneath each drip, and shift rugs, electronics and furniture clear of the wet area. Lay towels or plastic sheeting down to protect the floor.
  3. Drain a Bulging CeilingWhen water pools in a bubble overhead, pierce the low point with a screwdriver while standing clear, and let it run into a bucket.
  4. Leave the Roof AloneA wet roof and an unlit attic are both places to stay out of. Just observe the entry point from the room below, then hand the job to a trained crew.
  5. Document What You SeeBefore anything is moved or discarded, get video and photos of the attic, the ceiling and damaged items, and then phone us so drying can begin.
Insurance

What About Insurance?

Homeowners policies often cover interior water damage when a roof leak is sudden and accidental, such as wind tearing off shingles or a limb puncturing the roof. Leaks blamed on age, wear or skipped maintenance are usually denied, and the roof repair itself may be handled differently from the interior. Photos and moisture readings from us go to the adjuster, though only your policy decides coverage, and your agent can explain it.

The Usual Culprits

How It Usually Starts

  • Sun-cracked rubber collars where plumbing vent pipes come through the roof.
  • Skylight seals and curbs that dry out, crack and let rain run inside.
  • Fasteners working loose over time, so water follows the nail shank into the attic.
  • Leaves and grit collecting where two roof slopes meet, damming rain until it seeps through.
  • Holes and fractures left in the roof after a branch or hailstones hit it.
Why Speed Matters

What the Water Damages

Attic Insulation

Wet insulation mats down, stops insulating and holds water against the ceiling drywall for days. In some cases fiberglass batts dry out and stay. Removal is the usual answer for wet cellulose.

Mold in the Attic

Mold does well in an attic that is warm, dark and full of damp wood and drywall paper. Within 24 to 48 hours it can start growing, then move across the bottom of the roof deck.

Drywall Ceilings

Staining, sagging and loss of strength follow when drywall is not dried. Seams split, the tape lets go, and a heavy enough section can drop with little warning.

Rafters and Decking

Repeated wetting makes decking, trusses and rafters turn dark, swell up and go soft. Once they rot, the frame supporting the roof is weaker.
