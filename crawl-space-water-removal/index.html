Crawl Space Water Removal in West Palm Beach

Few homeowners notice right away when water collects beneath the floor. Most people find it when the floors feel cold and damp, a musty smell comes up through the vents, or someone opens the access hatch and sees a pond. By then the insulation is usually soaked and the wood above it has been wet for days. Crews from our company extract the water, haul away anything that cannot be saved and dry the structure ahead of rot.

Once the standing water is gone, the job is only halfway done. Wood framing takes water in gradually and gives it up just as gradually, which is why we meter the joists, beams and subfloor and leave dehumidifiers on until readings drop, typically 3 to 5 days. We also look for where the water came from, a leaking supply line, a drain, or groundwater after rain, so it does not fill back up.

Signs to Look For

  • Wood flooring on the first level that has begun to curl up along the seams
  • Batts of insulation drooping or dangling from the underside of the floor
  • A musty, earthy smell that is strongest near the floor vents
  • Flooring overhead that seems chilly, clammy, spongy or springy underfoot
  • Tide lines or white mineral crust on the piers and foundation walls
  • Rust on ductwork straps, pipe hangers or metal support posts
The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

First day

Water Pools Below

Low areas fill first while the ground underneath takes on water. Insulation starts wicking it up and humidity under the floor climbs fast.

Hours 24 to 48

Mold Gets Started

Growth may begin on wet framing and on the paper backing of the batts. Wet batts get heavy and begin to pull away from the subfloor.

Day 3 to 7

Moisture Moves Upstairs

Damp air rises into the living space. The lumber goes on taking in moisture, a stale smell spreads and flooring can begin to cup.

After a week

Rot Sets In

Lumber left damp this long starts breaking down. Expect softer framing, rusted fasteners, and a wet environment that draws in termites and similar pests.

Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

What You Breathe Upstairs

A good share of the air on your first floor rises from the crawl space, so mold spores and damp odors down there end up in the rooms you live in.

Pests Drawn to Moisture

Rodents, carpenter ants and termites all seek out moist lumber and soggy ground. Drying the space removes much of what attracts them.

Contaminated Water

What collects beneath a home is frequently dirty. If it came from a drain pipe or from waste laden groundwater it is rated Category 2 or 3, so we suit up and disinfect.

Wet Wiring and Ducts

Most crawl spaces carry wiring, junction boxes and HVAC ducts. Submerged wiring is a shock hazard and soaked duct insulation spreads musty air through the system.

What We Do

Our Crawl Space Water Removal Process

1

Inspect and Make Safe

We check for electrical hazards, sagging ducts and contaminated water before anyone crawls in. Then we map how far the water reached and take moisture readings on the joists and subfloor.

2

Remove Standing Water

Submersible pumps and extraction hoses remove the standing water, including the low spots and footing trenches where it collects. Any silt or mud sitting on the liner gets removed during this step.

3

Clear Out Ruined Material

Debris, saturated batts and any liner that cannot be reused leave the crawl space in bags. Where mold has begun or is likely, the bare joists and subfloor get cleaned and an antimicrobial applied.

4

Final Drying and Checks

Low profile air movers and a dehumidifier run under the house until the wood reaches a normal moisture reading. A final meter check follows, and you receive the numbers along with steps to prevent a repeat.
