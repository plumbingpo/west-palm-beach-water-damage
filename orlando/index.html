Where Water Damage Starts in Orlando Properties

Every house has a few weak points where water tends to get loose. These are the ones Orlando property owners report to us again and again.

Water heaters: Sediment collects in the bottom of a tank and slowly eats through the steel. Look for moisture at the base, rust streaks on the jacket or a small pool that keeps coming back. If the tank ruptures, it keeps refilling and draining onto the floor until someone closes the cold supply valve. Heaters in a hallway closet or attic can ruin flooring and ceilings well beyond the utility area.

Roofs: One strong storm is enough to peel back shingles and push water beneath the roof covering. Leaks also start around chimneys, vent pipes and skylights where the sealant has dried out. Since water follows the framing downhill, the ceiling stain rarely sits directly below the actual opening. For roof leaks in Orlando, we tarp where needed, pull out soaked insulation and dry the structure so the ceiling can be repaired.

Freezing: Cold nights, depending on the season, can break plumbing that showed no sign of trouble earlier. Outdoor spigots, sprinkler feeds and pipes in crawl spaces or along outside walls are hit first. A house left empty or a wing kept unheated faces the greatest risk. If a faucet only trickles on a freezing morning, shut off the water and call before the pipe thaws.

Backups: A drain that gurgles or several fixtures that slow down together point to a blockage in the main line. When the clog becomes complete, every flush and every load of laundry comes back out downstairs. The water is unsanitary, and hard surfaces need disinfecting after the solids and liquid are removed. Until a plumber opens the line, do not use sinks or toilets and keep everyone clear of the mess.

Seepage: Dampness at the base of a basement or foundation wall often means water is collecting outside. The usual reasons are grading that tilts inward, clogged perimeter drains and downspouts that empty too close. White chalky deposits, peeling masonry paint and a persistent damp smell are early warnings. The team at West Palm Beach Restoration can extract standing water and dry the space, and we will tell you what we think let it in.

A claim is decided on what can be shown, which makes documentation the most useful tool an Orlando owner has. Begin collecting it before the cleanup and before anything goes in the trash.

What to Do Before Our Orlando Crew Arrives

What you should do before we arrive depends on what kind of water you are looking at. Clean water from a supply pipe or a tap left running is the least risky. You can walk in it with shoes on, soak it up, and move belongings out of it. Water from a washing machine, dishwasher or aquarium is dirtier, so wear gloves and wash your hands afterward. Sewage and floodwater from outdoors are a health hazard, and the only job for you is to close the door and keep everyone out.

Whatever the type, a few things help our Orlando crew start faster. Make room for the truck near the entrance and clear the route from the front door to the damage. Note the time you discovered the problem and your best guess at the source. Have your insurance details at hand if you can reach them without trouble.

Do not assume clean water will remain that way. Left sitting in carpet and drywall, it picks up contamination and can support mold within 24 to 48 hours. That is why the opening hour counts, even when an Orlando leak seems small.
